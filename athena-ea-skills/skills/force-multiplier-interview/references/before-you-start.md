# Stage 0a — Brief the EA before doing anything

**Run this before any tool call.** Before searching their history, before
touching Fulcra, before the first interview question.

Three categories: things that must be resolved, things that need an explicit
yes, and things to state once and move past. Work them in that order.

Keep it to a few minutes. This is a briefing, not a consent form — read the
room and compress where the EA is clearly already across something.

---

## Blockers — resolve or explicitly acknowledge before proceeding

**1. The rubric has to be confirmed with their L1 manager.**

At Athena, review goals are pre-agreed with the L1 before self-review. This
pipeline works from a rubric of Performance (100%), Wellness & Development
(10%), Advocacy & Impact (10%), scored against the four Core Values — but
that's a structure, not the official criteria, and criteria change.

Ask whether they've confirmed the current Force Multiplier requirements,
format, and deadline. If not, tell them plainly: building against the wrong
rubric is the most expensive mistake available here, and one conversation
prevents it.

If they want to proceed anyway, get an explicit acknowledgment and note it in
state. Do not quietly continue.

**2. Confidentiality obligations.**

Client work will come up. Ask whether they have restrictions on what can go
into an AI tool or be stored outside their client's systems.

Then state your own rule: anonymize by default, never write client names,
financials, or internal document contents unless they put them there
themselves. "My client" and "a mid-size logistics firm" carry every argument
this deck needs.

---

## Consents — get an explicit yes for each

**3. Reading their past AI conversations.**

Say what this involves: searching their conversation history for work they've
done, opening the relevant ones, and extracting what they were doing and any
figures mentioned. Say why: it finds months of routine work they've stopped
being able to recall, which is the single biggest reason EAs undersell
themselves.

Say what you'll do with it: a summary written to their own Fulcra account.

**They can decline.** The pipeline runs interview-only, weaker but real.

**4. Creating a Fulcra account and storing work records.**

They'll be asked to set one up at Stage 2. State what goes in: a work log,
interview transcript, evidence base, and the deck. State whose it is: theirs,
not Athena's and not the agent's, and readable by their other agents.

Flag that you'll drive the setup — the account is created as part of signing
in, and their part is one browser step.

**5. The interview is adversarial on purpose.**

This matters and is easy to skip. Tell them the interview will reject vague
answers, demand arithmetic, refuse character claims in favour of incidents,
and at Stage 7 play a hostile, budget-constrained panel. Tell them it's
deliberate and that they can say "ease off" at any point.

An EA who isn't expecting this reads it as the agent being difficult.

---

## Disclosures — state once, then move on

**6. This takes more than one sitting.** Nine stages. The interview alone is
substantial, and Stage 3 depends on how much history there is. Tell them
progress is saved from Stage 2 onward so they can stop and resume.

**7. The evidence will be a floor, never a total.** It covers AI sessions and
whatever exports get ingested. Phone calls, in-person work, and anything done
without an agent are invisible. They need to be able to say this out loud in
the review room.

**8. Health, location, and calendar data are excluded.** If their Fulcra
account holds sleep, workout, or location records, none of it will be read or
used — including for the Wellness section, which is about sustainable working
practice rather than biometrics.

**9. You can't verify anything independently.** No access to Athena systems,
their client's systems, or any record of their work beyond what they provide
and what's mined. Every number traces back to them.

**10. This deck contains no ask.** It presents what they achieved against the
rubric — no salary figure, no request, no negotiation. Athena decides what
follows from it. If they want help thinking about a compensation conversation
separately, that's a different task from this one.

**11. This doesn't guarantee an outcome.** It's not Athena's official tool
and carries no standing with the review panel. It produces a better-evidenced
case than they'd otherwise have. That's the whole claim.

**12. Some of what surfaces may be unwelcome.** The process can reveal that a
Core Value has no evidence behind it, that a remembered figure doesn't hold
up, or that their current pace isn't sustainable. Naming that upfront is
fairer than letting it land as a surprise at Stage 5.

**13. Environment limits.** Export ingestion, statistical analysis, and the
manager dashboard need a Python environment — Claude Desktop or Cowork, not a
plain chat interface. Say which they're in and what that rules out, so it
isn't a disappointment three stages later.

---

## Close the brief

Ask one question: **"Anything there you want to change before we start?"**

Then go to Stage 0b and scope the run. Record in state: rubric confirmed or
acknowledged-unconfirmed, consent to history mining, consent to Fulcra, any
confidentiality constraints they named.

Do not repeat the brief on resumed sessions. Check state, and re-raise only
what's changed or what they declined and might now want.
