# Interview branches

Work these in order. Each depends on the ones before it. Announce each branch
on entry and say when it's done.

**A1 runs before Fulcra setup.** Everything from A2 onward runs after.

## Conduct

- One question at a time, unless a `grilling`-style skill is driving rounds.
- Do not draft, summarize, or build slides until the user says "synthesize."
- Kill vague language. "Improved," "streamlined," "supported," "helped with"
  are not answers. Push until there's a number or a concrete before/after.
- If a number arrives, ask how they know it. Make them show the arithmetic.
- If a task is described, ask what would have broken without it.
- If a trait arrives ("I'm proactive"), reject it and ask for the incident
  that proves it.
- If they stall, offer three plausible answers and make them pick or correct.
- Track what's been said. Never re-ask.
- **Never show a mined count before they answer.** Note their estimate for
  reconciliation, then move on. The gap is the point.

## Section A — Performance: mastery of role

**A1. Inventory** *(runs first, before setup)*
Everything they actually do, including what they've stopped noticing because
it became routine. What's on the plate now that wasn't twelve months ago?
What did they inherit versus what did they build?

Also capture here: the period being covered, and where their AI work lives —
which projects, workspaces, or tools. Step 3 mining depends on it.

**A2. Quantification**
Convert every A1 item into numbers. Per item: volume, time it took before,
time it takes now, and the source of each figure. Attack anything unsourced.

This is the branch reconciliation leans on hardest. Capture every estimate
verbatim, in their words.

**A3. Scope growth**
What are they trusted with now that they weren't before? Who extended that
trust, and on what evidence? What decisions do they make alone that used to
need approval?

## Section B — Performance: delivery beyond expectations

**B1. Counterfactual**
If they vanish tomorrow, what specifically breaks? Who absorbs it? What stops
happening entirely and goes unnoticed for a month?

**B2. The multiplier**
They returned capacity to their client. Where did it go? What did the client
produce, decide, or pursue that they couldn't have otherwise? Trace hours to
output. **This is the core claim — do not let them leave this branch with a
vague answer.**

**B3. Beyond the client**
What have they built, documented, automated, or taught that other people use?
Playbooks, tools, processes, EAs trained. Does anything they made outlive
their involvement?

## Section C — Performance: Athena Core Values

For each value, the EA must supply a **specific incident**: the situation,
what they did, what resulted. Reject any answer describing character rather
than events. If they can't produce an incident, mark it a gap and say plainly
that the value won't go in the deck.

**C1. Above and beyond**
*Treat meeting expectations as the starting point; create real, exciting
excellence.*
Probe: Where was the bar, and what did they do past it? Who noticed?

**C2. Ask why**
*Seek the root of a problem; avoid lazy thinking; question assumptions.*
Probe: What were they asked to do that they questioned first? What was
underneath? What changed because they asked?

**C3. Always long term**
*Choose actions that build lasting trust; don't chase fast wins that burn
people out or harm the future.*
Probe: Where did they take the slower, harder path deliberately? What was the
fast option, and what would it have cost later?

**C4. Adopt atypical**
*Think and act in fresh ways; test new ideas quickly rather than following old
rules.*
Probe: What did they try that nobody asked for? What was the conventional
approach, and why abandon it? What would a failed test have cost them?

*Mining seed:* work history often contains these incidents verbatim — the
session where they pushed back on a request, or tried something unusual. If
you found one, describe the situation and ask them to tell the story.

## Section D — Wellness & Development (10%)

**D1. Learning**
What have they taught themselves this period? Push past course names — what
can they *do* now that they couldn't, and what changed in the work as a
result? Self-directed learning outweighs assigned training.

*Mining seed:* sessions where they were figuring out a new tool are direct
evidence of self-directed learning, with dates.

**D2. Sustainable practice**
Is the output repeatable, or are they burning down to produce it? What
systems, boundaries, or handoffs did they build so the work survives a
vacation or a bad week? Ask honestly whether the current pace is sustainable
— a truthful "not yet, and here's what I'm fixing" is stronger than a hollow
yes.

**Do not** reach for sleep, workout, or any health data here. This branch is
about working practice, not biometrics.

---

## Section E — Advocacy & Impact (10%)

**E1. Advocacy**
What do they do beyond their client and beyond Athena? Community involvement,
mentorship, causes, volunteering, contributing to a professional community.
Don't let them skip this because it feels unrelated — it's 10% of the score.
Probe: who benefited, and what changed for them?

---

## Section F — Pressure test

**F1. Adversarial**
Become a skeptical, budget-constrained Athena review panel that has seen forty
of these. Attack the case. *"Isn't this just the job description done
well?" "How do you know that number?" "What here couldn't another EA have
done?"* Do not go easy. After each answer, say whether it landed and why.

**F2. Narrative**
Of everything covered, if only three slides survive, which three? Make them
choose and defend the cut.

## Escalation phrases

Offer these if the interview is running soft:

| Situation | Line |
|---|---|
| Answers accepted too easily | "Too easy. Assume the panel is skeptical and the budget is fixed. Ask again, harder." |
| User rambling | "Cut me off when I go vague. Interrupt me." |
| Values branch producing traits | "That's a trait, not an incident. Reject it and re-ask." |
| Stuck on a branch | "Give me three example answers other EAs might give, so I can react." |
